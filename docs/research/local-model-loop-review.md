# Vigil × ADR-0007: Verified-Trace SFT, Best-of-n, Jev Calibration, Typed-Decision Uses, and the Hosted-Trace Bridge (state as of 1 Nov 2026)

Bottom line: no published study shows that SFT/QLoRA on a few hundred verified traces beats best-of-n sampling at a matched inference budget. The published few-hundred-trace gains all start from weak bases and use stronger-teacher traces. ADR-0007 should therefore treat best-of-n with deployment-legal selection as the bar to beat, add a learning-curve design, and stop assuming that 300 local traces will produce a measurable win for an 8–9B student.

## TL;DR

- **SFT vs best-of-n:** Few-hundred-trace SFT gains (SWE-Gym: 491 traces, Qwen2.5-Coder-32B 7.0%→20.6% on SWE-bench Verified) come from weak 2024-era bases trained on GPT-4o/Claude 3.5 Sonnet traces.\[1\] Self-generated rejection-sampling fine-tuning gave +0.7 to +3 points. I found no matched-budget SFT vs best-of-n comparison. Keep the 300-trace gate as a pilot trigger, not as an expectation of a win.
- **Jev calibration:** I found no reliability diagram from TypeSafe. The best independent study (jev-1.13.0, 44 benchmarks) finds pooled ECE 0.047 but median per-benchmark ECE 0.168, caused by base-rate mismatch.\[2\] Vigil's 0.85/0.6 thresholds must be re-fitted per question type on owner labels. At $0.042 per million input tokens, labels cost more than inference.\[3\]
- **Firewall and hosted traces:** The firewall is legally necessary as well as hygienic. Anthropic, xAI and OpenAI terms all restrict training on outputs, and xAI's enterprise terms bar training any AI system.\[4\]\[5\]\[6\] Hosted traces may contribute only through an eval-only lane: hidden-test sources, planted-defect catalogs and difficulty labels. Mirror reviewer verdicts are self-grading and are not usable as labels.

## Executive summary

The open literature on SWE-agent SFT has moved fast. 8B models now reach 42.2% on SWE-bench Verified with about 18.1k trajectories, of which SWE-Lego's Table 1 counts 14.1k as fully resolved and 4.0k as only "semi-resolved" (partially correct) (arXiv 2601.01426). An independent reproduction got 31.8% with the same data (TIGER-Lab FIM-8B card). Nearly all of these gains come from thousands of traces generated by a much stronger teacher. Both SWE-Gym and SWE-smith publish data at the ~500-trace scale, and both trained weak 2024 Qwen2.5 bases on Claude/GPT-4o traces. Self-improvement on the model's own traces has been weak (SWE-Gym: "self-improvement is not yet working").\[7\] On-policy corrections from an expert help an 8B model (DAgger-style: 23.4%→29.8% Verified).

ADR-0007's E9 plan is really a distillation from the local Qwen3.6-35B-A3B into an 8–9B student. That move is legal because both are open-weight models, but the base gap is large: vendor-reported 73.4% Verified for the 35B, versus 31.8–42.2% for a fine-tuned 8B.\[8\]\[9\]\[10\] Best-of-n on the 35B is therefore the bar any 8B SFT must clear at matched tower-hours. ADR-0007's firewall is CONFIRMED by vendor terms.

Two clarifications are needed, and I recommend three ADR amendments:
1. Define provenance at the token level, because coordinator-written specs embedded in worker prompts are hosted text.
2. Add a `public-open` provenance class so the ADR can use permissively licensed open-weight-teacher datasets (Nemotron, Nebius) as a separate ablation arm.
3. Raise the eval split to at least 100 tasks so that the pre-registered McNemar test has power.

Jev is cheap and ranks well (median AUROC 0.886), but its calibration does not transfer across tasks.\[2\] Every typed decision that touches landing stays advisory.

## 1. Verified-trajectory SFT for small local models (state as of Nov 2026)

### 1.1 What the major releases actually show

**SWE-Gym (Pan et al., ICML 2025, arXiv 2412.21139).**
- The environment has 2,438 real task instances from 11 Python repositories.\[11\]
- Training data: 491 successful OpenHands trajectories sampled from GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet.\[1\]\[12\]
- Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct went from 7.0% to 20.6% on SWE-bench Verified (500 tasks) and from 3.0% to 15.3% on Lite.\[1\]\[7\] The 7B went from 1.8% to 10.6% on Verified.\[11\]
- The 32B agent plus a learned 32B verifier reached 32.0% Verified / 26.0% Lite at Best@16.\[1\] Best@k rose from 20.6@1 to 29.8@8 to 32.0@16 on a log-linear curve.\[13\]
- A LoRA-trained verifier (29.8@8) beat full-parameter fine-tuning (27.2@8), which the authors attribute to regularization.\[1\]
- The headline negative result: "Self-improvement is not yet working." Fine-tuning on a mixture of 868 on-policy and 491 off-policy trajectories did not beat off-policy alone in OpenHands.\[7\] Under the MoatlessTools scaffold, rejection-sampling fine-tuning (RFT) moved the 7B from 7.0% to 10.0% (two iterations) and the 32B from 19.0% to 19.7% on Lite.\[7\]\[11\]
- **Verdict vs ADR-0007: CONTRADICTS the implicit expectation** that a few hundred self-generated verified traces produce a large gain. The large gains in SWE-Gym came from stronger-teacher traces into a weak base.

**SWE-smith (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2025 D&B, arXiv 2504.21798).**
- 50k synthetic task instances from 128 repositories.\[14\]
- 5,016 expert trajectories from SWE-agent + Claude 3.7 Sonnet produced SWE-agent-LM-32B: 40.2% Verified, 30.7% Lite. The 7B (2k trajectories) reached 15.2% Verified and 11.7% Lite.\[15\]\[16\]
- At 500 trajectories the 32B reached 28.2% Verified. Performance rose monotonically from 14.3% at 100 trajectories to 40.2% at 5,016 (per the alphaXiv summary of the paper's Figure 1).\[15\]\[17\]
- Any single task is capped at 3 trajectories in the training set to limit sampling bias.\[18\]
- The authors report that SWE-agent-LM-32B is clearly subpar on SWE-bench Multilingual.\[19\]
- **Verdict: EXTENDS.** The curve exists, but it is a stronger-teacher curve.

**R2E-Gym (Jain et al., COLM 2025, arXiv 2504.07164).**
- 8.1K procedurally generated executable problems. The 4,578-problem subset is decontaminated by removing repositories that overlap SWE-bench.\[20\]
- R2E-Gym-32B reaches 34.4% pass@1 and (per author announcement) 64.4% Pass@Any on Verified. The 7B agent scores 19.0% (as tabulated in arXiv 2605.12913).\[21\]\[22\]
- A hybrid of execution-based and execution-free verifiers reaches 51.0% at Best@26 (49.4% at Best@16). The paper finds that "test-based verifiers suffer from low distinguishability, while execution-free verifiers are biased and often rely on stylistic features."\[20\]\[23\]

**SWE-Dev (arXiv 2506.07636).** Sampling yielded 17k unevaluated and 2.3k correct trajectories, of which 914 came from Nebius. SWE-Dev-32B reaches 36.6% Verified.\[24\]

**Skywork-SWE (arXiv 2506.19290).**
- 10,169 runtime-validated instances from 2,531 repositories, and 8,209 validated trajectories.\[25\]\[26\]
- Skywork-SWE-32B scores 38.0% pass@1 and 47.0% with test-time scaling.\[27\]
- The abstract describes "over 8,000 successfully runtime-validated training trajectories" with performance "showing no signs of saturation." The R² = 0.992 log-linear fit quoted by secondary summaries is not in the abstract, so treat it as unverified.

**Nebius SWE-rebench OpenHands trajectories (Dec 2025).**
- 67,074 trajectories over 1,823 Python repositories, generated by Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B-Instruct in OpenHands v0.54.0.\[28\]
- An RFT checkpoint of Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 reaches 50.3% Pass@1 and 68.3% Pass@5 on Verified at 500 turns. That matches Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B (50.0% / 63.0%), even though training trajectories were capped at 100 turns.\[29\]
- SWE-bench Verified issues were explicitly excluded from training.\[29\]

**NVIDIA Nemotron SWE sets.**
- Nemotron-SWE-v1: 59k OpenHands trajectories from Qwen3-Coder-480B (created 3 Dec 2025).\[30\]
- Nemotron-SFT-SWE-v2: 46,278 OpenHands trajectories plus 209,976 agentless samples; "ready for commercial use."\[31\]
- SWE-Zero: the nvidia/SWE-Zero-openhands-trajectories card lists "Total Trajectories: 318,115" from Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B-Instruct, licensed CC BY 4.0 and "ready for commercial/non-commercial use" (paper arXiv 2604.01496). Earlier commits of the same card gave 270k–350k.
- SWE-Hero: 34k trajectories.\[32\]
- A third-party embedding card describes Nemotron-SFT-SWE-v3 as 237,970 trajectories across OpenHands, SWE-agent, mini-SWE-agent and opencode harnesses.\[33\] This is secondary and unverified.
- The Nemotron 3 Super report filters SFT data by success and difficulty and judges trajectories at both outcome and process level with an LM judge.\[34\] Nemotron 3 Ultra (arXiv 2606.15007) drops "trajectories that edit but never run tests."\[35\]

**SWE-Lego (arXiv 2601.01426, Jan 2026).**
- 32,119 instances (18,409 real, 13,710 synthetic) and about 18.1k trajectories across 3,251 repos. Table 1 splits these into "14.1k resolved + 4.0k semi-resolved"; the semi-resolved ones "do not pass all test cases but have partial correctness."
- Qwen3-8B reaches 42.2% Verified with SFT only (49.6% at TTS@16). Qwen3-32B reaches 52.6% (58.8% at TTS@16).\[36\]
- For the 32B, Fig. 1b breaks the gain down as: hybrid dataset "+25.6%", refined SFT (error masking plus a difficulty curriculum) "+3.8%", and TTS "+6.2%", "together lifting the Qwen3-32B model from 23.2% to 58.8%." These increments are 32B figures, not 8B figures.
- The paper distinguishes "hack-free" results (42.2%) from a hackable setting (44.4%), using the latest Docker images.\[37\] This is evidence that git-history leakage inflates scores.
- **Reproducibility warning:** TIGER-Lab's FIM-8B card reports a reproduction of "Qwen3-8B + SWE-Lego" at 31.80% Verified and 27.30% Lite (mean of three seeds), about 10 points below the paper.\[9\]

**Post-mid-2026 additions.**
- **CoderForge-Preview (Together):** Qwen3-32B at 59.4% Verified. It used 8 trajectories per problem for R2E-Gym and SWE-rebench and 4 for SWE-smith, and filtered the Nebius set from 67,074 to 32,161.\[38\]
- **Scale-SWE (arXiv 2602.09892):** Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct from 22.0% to 64.0%.\[39\]
- **daVinci-Env/OpenSWE (arXiv 2603.13023):** 32B at 62.4%, 72B at 66.0%.\[40\]
- **DAgger-style training for Qwen3-8B (arXiv 2605.12913):**

  | Method | SWE-bench Verified | SWE-Gym holdout |
  |---|---|---|
  | SFT | 23.4% | 12.0% |
  | On-policy distillation | 26.2% | 16.0% |
  | DAgger-style | 29.8% | 19.0%\[21\] |

- **Multilingual:** SWE-Bench++ (arXiv 2512.17419) reports that 145 hard multilingual trajectories (2.8% of the mix) lifted a 7B on SWE-bench Multilingual from 5/300 to 11/300. Scaling that subset to 800 trajectories quadrupled the 7B baseline, and the 32B reached 25/300.\[41\]

**Not verified in this pass:** Multi-SWE-RL trace counts, and SWE-bench Pro results for 7–14B fine-tunes. I found no primary numbers, so treat any figures you have for them as unverified.

### 1.2 The local baseline you are actually up against

Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (released 16 April 2026, Apache 2.0) has these **vendor-reported** scores:
- 73.4% SWE-bench Verified
- 67.2% Multilingual
- 49.5% SWE-bench Pro
- 51.5% Terminal-Bench 2.0\[10\]\[42\]

These were measured with Alibaba's internal scaffold, not the public harness.\[10\] Your OpenHands/Claude Code harness on llama.cpp quants will score lower, so measure it yourself in E1.

The comparison that matters for E9 is a fine-tuned 8B against the 35B at matched tower-hours. The best published 8B SFT figure is 42.2% (31.8% reproduced).\[8\]\[9\] **Inference:** a 300-trace QLoRA 8B is very unlikely to beat the 35B's pass@1, let alone its best-of-n. E9 only makes sense if its goal is an 8B that is "cheaper per verified pass," which requires a cost-normalized bar.

### 1.3 The key question: does few-hundred-trace SFT beat matched best-of-n?

**There is no published matched-budget comparison.** The closest evidence is mixed:
- **Inference scaling looks stronger at the same base.** SWE-Gym's fine-tuned 32B gained more from Best@8 with a verifier (20.6→29.8) than it did from the 491-trace SFT itself (7.0→20.6, measured from a much weaker zero-shot starting point).\[11\]\[13\] SWE-Lego's 8B gains +7.4 from TTS@16 (abstract: "42.2%→49.6% and 52.6%→58.8% under TTS@16").
- **Own-policy RFT at small scale gives small gains:** +3.0 (7B) and +0.7 (32B) on Lite in SWE-Gym.\[11\]
- **Learning curves:** SWE-smith goes 100 → 500 → 5,016 trajectories as 14.3% → 28.2% → 40.2%.\[15\]\[17\] Skywork and SWE-Gym report log-linear trends.\[26\] On a log-linear curve, going from 0 to 300 traces is the steep part, but only from a weak base.

**Verdict on the 300-trace threshold: EXTENDS/AMENDS.** 300 is in the range where published gains appear, but only for teacher-trace SFT into weak bases. For own-model (35B→8B) traces, expect single-digit gains and pre-register that expectation.

### 1.4 Own-repo and on-policy vs synthetic

**Real vs synthetic.** Scale-SWE ran controlled SFT on SWE-Gym vs SWE-smith and found that the larger synthetic SWE-smith "yields slightly inferior performance," concluding that real data beats massive synthetic data.\[39\] SWE-Lego's best result comes from a hybrid of the two.

**On-policy.** DAgger-style training (on-policy states with expert relabeling) beats plain SFT by 6.4 points at 8B. SWE-Gym found that on-policy data helped the verifier but not the policy.

**Own-repo traces.** I found no published study isolating own-repo traces against public synthetic traces. **Inference:** own-repo verified traces should mostly help in-distribution, i.e. on Vigil's own eval split, so the held-out split must contain repos and tasks that never appear in the train collection.

### 1.5 QLoRA feasibility (inferred)

Thinking Machines' LoRA primer reports that LoRA matches full fine-tuning for SL on small datasets when applied to all weight matrices including MLP/MoE layers, with a learning rate 20–100× higher.\[43\] FireAct (arXiv 2310.05915) found full FT beat LoRA on Llama-2-7B (30.2 vs 26.2 EM).\[44\] QLoRA on an 8–9B model with 2×16 GB fits for short sequences. Agentic traces of 32k+ tokens will need gradient checkpointing and probably truncation or turn-level chunking (**inference**).

## 2. Best-of-n as both baseline and data multiplier

### 2.1 Metrics: pass@k, best@k, pass^k

- **pass@k:** at least one of k samples passes. This is an oracle upper bound.\[13\]
- **Best@k:** the sample chosen by a selector passes. This is what deployment gets (SWE-Gym's definitions).\[13\]
- **pass^k:** all k trials pass. This is a reliability metric (τ-bench, Yao et al., arXiv 2406.12045, June 2024).\[45\]\[46\]

With independent trials at per-trial success p, pass@k = 1−(1−p)^k and pass^k = p^k.\[47\] In τ-bench, gpt-4o with function calling scored pass^1 ≈ 61% on retail but pass^8 < 25%.\[48\]

Vigil should report all three. Best@k is the baseline; pass@k is the data-collection yield, because it counts tasks with at least one verified trace. pass^k is the flakiness and robustness signal.

### 2.2 Optimal n for 7–35B agents

The published curves are log-linear and flatten slowly:
- SWE-Gym: Best@k 20.6@1 → 29.8@8 → 32.0@16.\[13\]
- R2E-Gym hybrid: 49.4@16 → 51.0@26.\[20\]
- SWE-Lego 8B: 42.2 → 49.6 at TTS@16.\[49\]

Most of the gain arrives by n = 8. **Inference for Vigil:** use n = 8 for the baseline and the collection, with k ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8} reported from the same samples. Going to n = 16 roughly doubles tower time for about 2–3 more points.

### 2.3 Selection policies

- **Execution-based selection** (agent-generated reproduction tests plus regression tests) suffers from low distinguishability: many candidates pass the same tests (R2E-Gym).\[23\]
- **Execution-free critics** are biased toward style (R2E-Gym). A hybrid adds 7–8 points over either alone.\[23\]\[50\]
- **Generative verifiers** "consistently outperform regression-based scorers" in SWE-Lego. The same paper finds that "sequential scaling saturates around 100–140 turns; beyond this point, compute is better allocated to parallel rollouts."
- **Heterogeneous pools** help. LLM-as-a-Verifier (arXiv 2607.05391) selected from 3 frontier-model candidates (mean 76.1%, oracle 84.4%) to reach 78.2%.\[51\]

**Critical rule for Vigil (inference):** the best-of-n baseline must select using only deployment-legal signals: worker-visible tests, the reviewer, and an optional critic. If Vigil selects by hidden tests, the baseline becomes pass@k, which inflates it and makes SFT look worse than it is. Hidden tests are used only for scoring.

### 2.4 Keeping best-of-n honest

**Inferred protocol, consistent with the sources above:**
- Pre-register temperature, top-p, max turns, context cap, quant and llama.cpp build hash.
- Fix seeds per (task, sample index).
- Run k ≥ 3 independent repeats of pass@1 to estimate run-to-run variance before claiming any delta.
- Freeze prompts by digest.

SWE-Gym itself sampled at temperatures 0 and 1 and got different yields: GPT-4o on SWE-Gym Full gave 111 successes (4.55%) at temperature 0 versus 188 (7.71%) at temperature 1.\[13\] Temperature therefore materially changes both baseline and yield.

### 2.5 Filtering best-of-n samples for SFT

- **Verified-pass only:** keep traces that pass hidden tests in the single-commit checkout. This CONFIRMS ADR-0007, and SWE-Lego's hack-free/hackable gap supports the fresh-checkout design.
- **De-duplicate and cap per task:** SWE-smith caps at 3 trajectories per task.\[18\]
- **Difficulty balance:** Nemotron 3 Super drops easy tasks, and SWE-Lego uses a difficulty curriculum (+3.8 combined with error masking).\[8\]
- **Process audit:** Nemotron 3 Ultra drops trajectories that edit but never run tests.\[35\] Add a hackability audit for test edits, skip markers and assertion weakening, using the Opus auditor.
- **Error masking:** mask failed tool calls from the loss rather than dropping whole traces (SWE-Lego).\[52\]

## 3. Jev calibration

### 3.1 What TypeSafe publishes

**Primary claims.** Jev launched in early access on 15 September 2026. TypeSafe describes it as trained with "Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions (RLCD)."\[3\] It returns Choice/Score answers with a confidence value, and Noul answers as a bare P(yes) with no separate confidence (OpenRouter Jev docs; docs.typesafe.ai/confidence).\[53\]\[54\]\[55\]

**What is missing.** Independent reviewers (victordibia.com explainer; AgentConn) report that TypeSafe publishes no reliability diagram, ECE or Brier score. The only accuracy-by-confidence figure is 27 of 30 correct above 0.9 confidence, on an older model (95% CI 74–97%).\[56\]\[57\]

**Conflict.** A pentest-harness paper (arXiv 2609.28940) states that "TypeSafe reports ECE of 0.246 for Jev on their own evaluation set."\[58\] I could not locate this in TypeSafe's own docs, so treat it as unconfirmed.

**Discard.** A vercel.app article claiming Jev "7B/14B" ECE of 0.014–0.018 has no provenance and should be ignored.\[59\]

### 3.2 Independent calibration evidence

**"Just Ask Jev" (arXiv 2609.29429; jev-1.13.0; 44 benchmarks; 7,193 instances).**
- Median AUROC 0.886 zero-shot.\[2\]
- Pooled ECE 0.047, but median per-benchmark ECE 0.168 against a perfect-calibration null of 0.074. 24 of 31 benchmarks exceed the null's 95th percentile.\[2\]
- The cause is base-rate mismatch: the mean probability misses the positive rate by a median of 0.125.\[2\]
- A threshold fitted on 10 labels lifts F1 where Jev fires too rarely.\[2\]
- Keeping the most-confident half raises median accuracy from 0.793 to 0.933.\[2\]

**Radiology factuality (arXiv 2609.27607).**
- Raw ECE 0.1192. Isotonic calibration reduced holdout ECE from 0.1235 to 0.0077.\[60\]
- 0% of judgments were identical across 5 runs (rank consistency 0.986–0.990). Jev is not bit-deterministic.\[60\]

**Small benchmarks:**

| Benchmark | N | Accuracy | ECE | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tool-call risk (webofmike, 17 Sept 2026) | 60 | 91.7% | 0.0712 (latest), 0.0505 (preview) | 50 of 60 predictions in one bin |
| jev-phishing-bench | 2,000 | 62.6% | 0.154 | vs Claude Haiku 4.5's 0.097 |
| Prompt injection | 662 | — | 0.0588 | —\[56\]\[61\]\[62\] |

**Verdict:** Jev's probabilities rank well, but they do not transfer as thresholds across tasks. Vigil's 0.85/0.6 thresholds match TypeSafe's confidence-gated routing pattern (act above 0.85, confirm 0.6–0.85, hand off below 0.6).\[63\] TypeSafe's own Confidence page instead suggests acting above 0.9 and escalating below 0.5.\[3\]\[55\] Neither set is validated for Vigil's question types.

### 3.3 Platform documentation (Decisions API and Workers AI)

**OpenRouter (Jev docs page, current):**
- Endpoint: `POST /api/alpha/decisions`. This is an alpha endpoint.\[64\]
- `typesafe/jev-1.14` is current, `~typesafe/jev-latest` tracks the newest release, and `typesafe/jev-1.13` "is still available."\[54\]
- Context: 262,144 tokens. Requests over about 32,000 bytes of text run in "long-context mode, which TypeSafe says is slower and less accurate."\[54\]
- Pricing: output tokens are free; every response carries `usage.cost`. The jev-1.13 model page lists $0.042 per million input tokens.\[3\]\[54\]\[65\]
- Thresholds: "Probabilities differ between models on the same question, so pick thresholds again for each model."\[54\]\[66\]\[67\]
- OpenRouter requires non-null instructions and descriptions for Noul outcomes (Hackage `jev` client).\[64\]

**Cloudflare:**
- The `typesafe/jev` catalog page describes Jev 1.13 with a 32,000-token context window, "Zero data retention: Yes," and $0.042/M input, $0 cached input, $0 output.\[68\]
- A separate `typesafe/jev-1.14.0` page exists.\[69\]
- Jev on Cloudflare is a third-party model billed through AI Gateway credits. It returns HTTP 503 `UPSTREAM_BILLING_REQUIRED` when credits run out.\[70\]
- **Pinning risk:** the unversioned `typesafe/jev` ID is not a pin. Use the versioned ID.

**Aliases and rounding.** jev-latest and jev-preview move as new versions ship, so pin jev-1.14.0 or jev-1.13.0. Jev rounds probabilities to two decimals (livekit-plugins-typesafe).\[71\] As of 20 September 2026 the versioned 1.13.0 ID was accepted after the alias moved, but no retention period for old versions is stated (arXiv 2609.29429).\[2\]

**Option limits.** Score supports 2–10 levels (datanorth summary of TypeSafe docs).\[63\] I found no documented Choice option cap.

**Alternatives.** Cloudflare Clef (1 October 2026, Apache 2.0) reports a median latency of 209 ms against Jev's 524 ms (Cloudflare-reported). OpenRouter lists 22 decision models from 13 makers as of 16 October 2026.\[54\]\[72\]

### 3.4 Threshold-policy literature

Selective prediction (Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017, as applied in arXiv 2609.29429) supports acting on confident cases and routing the rest.\[2\] Post-hoc temperature or isotonic scaling fixes most miscalibration but needs task labels. TypeSafe's docs say thresholds "depend on your domain" and recommend plotting confidence against accuracy.\[73\]\[74\] jev-eval refuses to recommend thresholds below 50 labels and warns when ECE exceeds 0.1.\[67\]

### 3.5 Continuous calibration-monitoring design (recommended; inferred from the above)

1. **Slices.** Key by (question_id digest, primitive type, model pin, deployment surface). Never pool across question types, because the pooled-vs-per-benchmark gap above is exactly what pooling hides.
2. **Labels.** The owner labels a stratified sample: 100% of escalated-to-Haiku cases, plus 10–20% of act-band and wait-band decisions.
3. **Minimum counts before trusting the act threshold (Wilson 95% lower bound ≥ 0.85; my arithmetic):**

   | Observed act-band precision | Labels needed |
   |---|---|
   | 95% | ≈ 60 |
   | 90% | ≈ 250–300 |

   Below 50 labels per slice, run in shadow mode only (the jev-eval floor).\[67\]
4. **Metrics.**
   - Rolling Brier and adaptive-bin ECE, reported alongside its noise floor at the current n.
   - Act-band precision and its Wilson lower bound.
   - Base-rate drift: mean p minus label rate, which is the documented failure mode.
5. **Alarms.**
   - Act-band Wilson lower bound below 0.85 over the last 100 labels.
   - |mean p − positive rate| above 0.10.
   - ECE above 0.10 (the jev-eval guard).\[67\]
6. **Version bumps.** Shadow-run the new pin on the same decisions until each slice has at least 60 labels, then re-fit thresholds. Thresholds never carry over between pins or surfaces.\[66\]\[75\]
7. **Determinism.** Log the rounded p and the request hash. Treat changes of ±0.01–0.02 between identical calls as noise.

## 4. Typed-decision uses beyond the router, ranked by evidence-to-cost

**Cost basis:** $0.042 per million input tokens and free output.\[3\]
- A 3k-token state costs about $0.00013 per decision. 1,000 decisions cost about $0.13.
- A 20k-token state costs about $0.00084.
- Keep states under about 32 KB to avoid long-context mode.

**Haiku escalation cost** depends on which Haiku the decider escalates to. Anthropic's current pricing docs still list Claude Haiku 4.5 at $1/$5 per million tokens, so a 5k-in/300-out escalation costs about $0.0065, roughly 50× a Jev call. The same page lists Claude Haiku 5.5 at $0.10/$0.50 (prompts up to 100k tokens), which brings that escalation to about $0.00065, roughly 5× a Jev call (my arithmetic).

**Ranking.**

1. **Flake-vs-regression triage. Highest evidence, ADVISORY.**
   - *Academic evidence.* These are trained, feature-based classifiers, not zero-shot models:
     - FlakeFlagger (ICSE 2021; 24 Java projects; 22,245 tests run 10,000×; 811 flaky tests): precision 60%, recall 74%, F1 66%, AUC 86%.\[76\]
     - Flakify (TSE 2022, CodeBERT): precision 70% / recall 90% on the FlakeFlagger dataset; 99% / 96% on IDoFT.\[77\]
   - *Industry practice is rerun-based.*
     - Google's Flakiness Debugger reruns each test 50× and was useful for 68 of 83 flaky-test issues (82%). Flaky tests caused 4.56% of TAP failures.\[78\]
     - Microsoft CloudBuild found 4.6% of distinct tests flaky over 30 days. 86% of flaky tests were flaky only in CI.\[79\]
     - Meta's PFS is a Bayesian score with no published precision.\[80\]
   - *Expected precision at p ≥ 0.85 (inference):* 0.7–0.9, unverified for Jev. FlakeFlagger's top features are execution time and coverage, which Jev cannot see unless they are serialized into the state.\[76\]
   - *If wrong:* a real regression gets quarantined as a flake.
   - *Rule:* Jev orders the rerun queue. Quarantine is decided by reruns plus history.

2. **Spec-gate: "is this done_when testable?" Cheap and low-risk.**
   - No direct literature. "Just Ask Jev" shows that generic Noul questions rank well.\[2\]
   - *If wrong:* a weak spec gets through (caught later by the verifier), or a good one gets bounced to the owner.
   - Allowed to route (ask the owner), never to reject.

3. **Review-finding severity scoring. Moderate.**
   - I gathered no primary severity-prediction numbers in this pass. Jev's ordinal Score with a soft readout beat Noul on 19 of 31 benchmarks in arXiv 2609.29429. Use the expected level, never the argmax (argmax readouts "lose almost everywhere").\[2\]
   - *If wrong:* a critical finding is ranked low. Mitigate by making the Opus auditor's findings un-downgradable.

4. **Card recommendation confidence. Low evidence, low risk.** Use it purely for ordering.

5. **Merge-gate criterion scoring. Highest risk, weakest fit.**
   - The state (JUnit diff plus acceptance output) is long and relational, which is exactly where Jev's documented weaknesses lie: "indirect meaning and adversarial content," and references missing from the state.\[2\]
   - Base-rate mismatch means p ≥ 0.85 does not imply 85% precision.\[2\]
   - *If wrong:* an unmet criterion lands.
   - **Must remain advisory.**

**Advisory-only under the rule that "nothing a model says decides landing":** merge-gate criterion scoring, flake quarantine, and severity downgrades. Spec-gate and card confidence may route work but must not block or land it.

## 5. The hosted-trace bridge

### 5.1 Terms (as of the sources found, 2026)

- **Anthropic.**
  - The Commercial Terms bar using the Services "to build a competing product or service, including to train competing AI models."\[4\]
  - The Usage Policy bars using outputs "to train an AI model (e.g., 'model scraping' or 'model distillation') without prior authorization."\[4\]
  - Anthropic's help article (16 March 2026) allows training non-competing models. It lists "models designed for open-ended text generation" and "using Outputs as training targets for models" as prohibited.\[81\]
  - A local coding agent is plausibly "competing" (**inference**).
- **xAI.**
  - The Enterprise terms say the customer will not "use any Output to train any foundation models, large language models, or other artificial intelligence systems except as may be expressly permitted in an Order Form."\[6\]
  - The consumer-side terms bar training "a model that is competitive with the Service."\[82\]
- **OpenAI.** The Terms of Use prohibit using "Output to develop models that compete with OpenAI."\[5\]
- **Cursor.** Terms not verified in this pass. Cursor routes to third-party models whose own terms then apply (**inference**).\[82\]

**Verdict (a): distillation from Claude, Grok or OpenAI outputs into the local model is not terms-permitted without authorization.** The firewall is CONFIRMED.

### 5.2 Is stronger-model distillation empirically better?

Yes, at small scale:
- SWE-Gym's 491 GPT-4o/Claude traces gave +13.6 points, while own-policy RFT gave +0.7 to +3.\[11\]
- SWE-smith used Claude 3.7 traces.\[83\]
- DAgger-style expert relabeling beat SFT at 8B.\[21\]

**Verdict (b): better, but legally unavailable from hosted vendors.** Permissively licensed open-weight-teacher datasets are the legal substitute. Nemotron-SFT-SWE-v2 is "ready for commercial use," and the Nebius and SWE-Lego traces come from Qwen3-Coder-480B.\[28\]\[31\]\[36\] Check each dataset's licence (**inference**).

### 5.3 Minimal provenance-preserving contributions (eval-only lane)

- **Eval-only cases:** hosted-solved tasks become eval-split items scored only by hidden tests. Hosted patches are never shown to local workers.
- **Hidden-test sources:** hosted models can draft hidden tests. Tests are not training targets and the worker never sees them. Risk: tests encode the hosted model's solution shape (**inference**).
- **Planted-defect catalogs:** hosted-generated defects seed reviewer-ladder evaluations.
- **Difficulty labels:** hosted pass/fail labels stratify the curriculum and eval. A label is a scalar, not text (**inference**: low terms risk, but Anthropic's "outputs as training targets" language argues against using them as loss targets).

**Contamination.** Decontamination by repository is standard practice (R2E-Gym's subset, Nebius's exclusions).\[29\] I found no study specific to hosted-output-derived eval items for local models. **Inference:** keep every hosted-derived item in a separately scored lane, never in the McNemar bar.

### 5.4 Mirror reviewer: usable labels or self-grading?

It is self-grading:
- LLM evaluators recognize their own generations, and self-recognition correlates linearly with self-preference (Panickssery, Bowman & Feng, NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2404.13076).\[84\]\[85\]
- Stronger models' self-preference is mostly legitimate, but harmful self-preference persists exactly when they are wrong (arXiv 2504.03846).\[86\]\[87\]
- Shared-conversation review adds anchoring to the builder's rationale (**inference**).

Mirror verdicts are not labels. Use them only where the verifier independently disagrees, as a catalog of "reviewer missed it" cases.

### 5.5 Token-level provenance (ADR gap; inferred)

Vigil's coordinator, presumably Claude, writes the task cards that appear inside local worker prompts. Under the literal rule "anything containing hosted-model text is excluded," every local trace would be excluded.

Amend the ADR so that provenance is computed on loss-bearing tokens, and require train-split cards to be human- or local-authored. That way no hosted text appears even as input. Re-check this against Anthropic's Usage Policy wording, which covers "inputs and outputs."\[88\]

## Recommended experiment order against ADR-0007

**Assumptions (inferred, not measured):**
- About 20 tower-minutes per local agent attempt, i.e. 3 attempts per hour.
- An eval split of at least 100 tasks.
- Reviewer cost of about $0.12 per Sonnet review and $0.23 per Opus audit, computed at $3/$15 and $5/$25 per million tokens (the Opus 4.5–5 tier). Anthropic's current pricing docs list Claude Sonnet 5.5 at $2/$10 and Claude Opus 5.5 at $4/$20. At those prices the same reviews cost about $0.08 and $0.18, so the hosted-$ figures in the table below are upper bounds, about 20–33% high (my arithmetic).

| Step | Design | Attempts | Tower-hours | Hosted $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E0 smoke | 30 eval tasks, n = 1; harness and verifier sanity check, plus k = 3 repeats on 10 tasks | 60 | ≈ 20 | Reviewer on passes only, ≈ $5 |
| E1 best-of-n baseline | ≥ 100 eval tasks, n = 8; report pass@1/2/4/8, Best@8 (deployment-legal selector) and pass^3; pre-registered sampling | 800 | ≈ 270 | Reviewer on selected candidates, ≈ $12–35 |
| E2 train collection | Train split, n = 8 until ≥ 300 verified, audit-clean, local traces (cap 3 per task, dedupe, drop edit-without-test) | ≈ 1,500–3,000 at a 10–20% yield | ≈ 500–1,000 | Opus audit of kept traces, ≈ $70 |
| E3 learning curve | QLoRA 8–9B on 100/200/300 subsets, 3 seeds each | — | ≈ 30–60 training plus evaluation | — |
| E4 held-out bar | Paired McNemar at p < 0.05: 8B-SFT vs 8B-base Best@n at matched tower-hours, and vs 35B pass@1 | 2 × 100 × n | ≈ 70–270 | — |
| Jev monitoring | Shadow mode until 60 labels per slice | — | — | < $1 per 1,000 decisions |

**McNemar power (my arithmetic):** an exact test with 12 discordant pairs split 10:2 gives p ≈ 0.039. On a 60-task split that requires about a +13-point net gain. Use at least 100 eval tasks.

## Explicit ADR-0007 callouts

- **300-trace threshold: AMEND.** Keep it as the trigger, and add a 100/200/300 learning curve plus a pre-registered expected effect size. The published "few hundred traces" wins are teacher-to-weak-base results.
- **Firewall: CONFIRMED,** with two amendments:
  1. Token-level provenance, covering hosted specs in prompts.
  2. A new `public-open` provenance class for permissively licensed open-weight-teacher datasets, as a separate ablation arm, never mixed into `local`.
- **8–9B retarget: CONFIRMED as feasible, CONTRADICTED as a likely win.** The bar must be cost-normalized against 35B best-of-n.
- **Best-of-n baseline: EXTENDS.** Selection must use deployment-legal signals only; hidden tests are for scoring only.
- **Sequence (smoke → best-of-n → collection → SFT): CONFIRMED,** with a learning-curve step inserted before the held-out bar.

## Do-not-adopt list

- Jev as a landing gate for merge criteria, quarantine or severity downgrades.
- Vigil's 0.85/0.6 thresholds without per-slice owner labels.
- Pooled ECE, or vendor or uncited ECE claims (for example, the "0.018" vercel.app figure).
- Unversioned IDs: `typesafe/jev` and `~typesafe/jev-latest`.
- Training on Claude, Grok, OpenAI or Cursor-mirror traces, including scalar labels used as loss targets.
- Mirror reviewer verdicts as review labels.
- Hidden-test selection inside the best-of-n baseline.
- Comparing local results to the vendor-reported 73.4% Verified.\[10\]
- Expecting RFT self-improvement gains above a few points below 1k traces.

## Caveats

- Many 2026 figures are single-source arXiv preprints or vendor claims: Qwen3.6 scores, Clef latency, and the TypeSafe ECE of 0.246.
- The SWE-Lego 8B result failed to reproduce by about 10 points.\[9\]
- Licences for several datasets, Cursor's terms, Multi-SWE-RL, SWE-bench Pro small-model numbers and severity-prediction literature were not verified in this pass.
- Anthropic prices were checked against its current pricing docs, but the table's hosted-$ figures use the older Sonnet/Opus rates and are upper bounds. Tower-hour figures are estimates. Measure both in E0.
